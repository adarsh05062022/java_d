# Downstream utility of GRDM synthetic data (XGBoost)

Generated by `downstream/report.py`. Every regime is trained with identical, untuned
XGBoost hyperparameters and evaluated on the **same real held-out test set**. The
synthetic-only regime is subsampled to the real training row count, so `real` vs
`synthetic only` compares data *quality*; the mixed rows then add quantity on top of the
full real set. Parenthesised numbers are the change against the real-only baseline,
sign-corrected so positive always means better.

### What the numbers say

**The two databases behave completely differently, and the fidelity metrics did not
predict the size of the gap.** California synthetic data is a near-drop-in replacement for
real data; Berka synthetic data is close to useless on its own. GRDM’s reproduced
fidelity scores differ far less dramatically between the two (Column Shapes 98.60 vs 96.89,
1-HOP 96.48 vs 91.43), so a few points of fidelity separate regimes that downstream utility
separates by an order of magnitude. California is 2 tables and 1 relationship; Berka is 8
tables and 9 relationships with a multi-parent `disp`.

**The leakage-free rerun confirms the interim Berka result rather than overturning it.**
Retraining the generator on `split_train` alone moves synthetic-only AUROC from 0.5798 to
0.5917 on loan default and from 0.6094 to 0.6021 on card ownership -- both well inside the
seed spread. Training on the test rows was not what made this synthetic data look bad; the
generator simply does not carry these relationships. Note the real-only baselines are
identical across the two protocols by construction: both evaluate the same fixed split, and
only what the generator was allowed to see differs.

**Mixing is safe but not useful.** Adding synthetic rows on top of the full real training
set moves every primary metric by at most 0.034 in either direction, on both databases and
at every mixing ratio. There is no augmentation win here -- but also no damage, which is
the more useful practical finding when synthetic data is added for privacy or volume
reasons rather than accuracy ones.

**The one place mixing does help is minority-class recall.** On the leakage-free Berka loan
task, adding synthetic data lifts macro-F1 by up to +0.038 and balanced accuracy by +0.031
while costing -0.034 AUROC at the full mixing ratio. Synthetic rows act like minority
oversampling: they move the decision threshold without adding ranking information.

**Berka fails in two distinct ways, and only one of them is visible in the marginals.**
For loan default the marginal itself breaks -- in the leakage-free run the default rate
falls from 0.112 to about 0.073 and class B collapses from 24 real rows to 1-6 synthetic.
For card ownership the marginal is nearly exact (0.168 real vs 0.160-0.167 synthetic) and
the task still collapses from 0.874 to 0.602 AUROC, because what is lost is the
*cross-table dependency* between account behaviour and card ownership, not the rate.
Matching per-column distributions is therefore not sufficient for downstream utility, which
is precisely the claim a column-shape metric cannot test.

**Watch accuracy.** On both Berka tasks the synthetic-only model roughly matches the real
model on plain accuracy (0.8628 vs 0.8655 on leakage-free loan default) while sitting at
chance on balanced accuracy (0.500-0.515). It achieves that by predicting the majority class
almost everywhere. Any evaluation reporting accuracy alone would have scored this synthetic
data as excellent.

**Root cause and fixes.** Three mechanisms were diagnosed and five fixes tried; see
`DIAGNOSIS.md` and `FIXES.md` for the full analysis and the table directly below. In short:
conditioning depth (`n_hops`) is the binding constraint for the cross-table dependency task,
gradient starvation on small tables is the binding constraint for the single-parent task, and
the categorical-encoding pathology -- though real and reproducible -- turned out not to
matter for downstream utility at all. Combining deeper conditioning with balanced seed
sampling (S5) gives the best result on the dependency task and recovers most of what depth
alone costs on the other.
