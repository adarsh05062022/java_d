# Why Berka fails: root-cause diagnosis

Generated by `downstream/diagnose.py`. Companion to `RESULTS_downstream.md`, which
measures *that* Berka synthetic data is unusable (20-27% of the real signal retained);
this file establishes *why*.

## A2 — The conditioning window is the binding constraint

GRDM's GNN has `n_hops` layers, so while a row is generated it sees its own noisy
features plus its direct neighbours' raw columns, and nothing further. The table below
scores XGBoost on **real** data restricted to exactly that window. If the restricted real
score lands near the synthetic-on-everything score, then the generator is not failing to
model what it sees -- it simply never sees the rest.

| task | conditioning depth | features | real AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| berka_loan_default | own row only | 6 | 0.6015 ± 0.0131 |
| berka_loan_default | + 1-hop parents (= GRDM default) | 9 | 0.5561 ± 0.0087 |
| berka_loan_default | + 2-hop (full) | 97 | 0.8886 ± 0.0116 |
| berka_card_ownership | own row only | 1 | 0.6038 ± 0.0000 |
| berka_card_ownership | + 1-hop parents (= GRDM default) | 8 | 0.6708 ± 0.0016 |
| berka_card_ownership | + 2-hop (full) | 90 | 0.8739 ± 0.0013 |

Measured synthetic-only AUROC for comparison (now 3 generator seeds each, mean ± sd; see
`FIXES.md` for the full scoreboard including S3 and S5):

| task | baseline (n_hops=1) | S2 (n_hops=2) | S3 (balanced seeds, n_hops=1) | S5 (both, 1 seed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| berka_loan_default | 0.5917 ± 0.044 | 0.5264 ± 0.122 | **0.6613 ± 0.035** | 0.6322 |
| berka_card_ownership | 0.6021 ± 0.017 | 0.6687 ± 0.017 | 0.6164 ± 0.050 | **0.6813** |

**Card ownership reads exactly as H1 predicts.** The default generator (n_hops=1) lands at 0.60,
matching the *own-row-only* ceiling of 0.604 — not the 1-hop ceiling. Raising the depth to 2 moves
it to 0.67–0.69, matching the *1-hop* ceiling of 0.671. The effective conditioning window sits one
hop behind the nominal depth, which makes sense: a neighbour's features are themselves still noisy
while the row is generated. Reaching the full 0.874 would need the 2-hop aggregate window, i.e.
`n_hops=3`.

**Loan default is at its ceiling already, and depth alone makes it worse, not neutral.** Its
own-row and 1-hop windows score only 0.60/0.56 — there is nothing to recover at this depth — but
S2's 3-seed mean (0.5264) is *below* the 1-hop ceiling and below baseline, not merely flat. All of
the task's signal (0.89) lives in the transaction aggregates two hops away, so `n_hops=2` should be
neutral here at worst; instead it costs 0.065 AUROC. The likely mechanism, laid out in `FIXES.md`,
is that 2-hop conditioning widens every table's effective batch and further dilutes `loan`'s
already-thin 2.6-seeds-per-batch share. **S3's gradient rebalancing (below) recovers most of this
loss when combined with S2 (S5: 0.6322)**, which supports that reading. With only 19 positives in
the loan test set this column stays noisy regardless; treat magnitudes as indicative.

The practical consequence: **on Berka, downstream signal lives 2 hops from the row being
generated, but depth and gradient balance are coupled — adding depth without rebalancing seed
sampling pays for the one task by damaging the other.**

## A1 — The ordinal-decoding effect is real but column-specific, not systematic

Categorical columns enter `x` as raw integer codes and are Gaussian-diffused, then
decoded with `np.round`. A class is therefore reachable only through a band of the real
line, and `QuantileTransformer(output_distribution="normal")` shrinks that band in
proportion to the class's frequency. The prediction was that a rare class sandwiched
between dense neighbours loses mass while an equally rare class at the edge of the code
range survives.

**That prediction does not survive testing as a general law** — see the stratified medians
below. It holds sharply for a handful of columns and not at all across the rest.

Worst-hit rare classes (real share < 10%), mean over 3 generator seeds:

| table.column | code | position | real share | synth share | retained | normal-space band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| trans.k_symbol | 4 | interior | 0.0015 | 0.0002 | 0.14x | 0.004 |
| client.year | 71 | interior | 0.0065 | 0.0015 | 0.23x | 0.603 |
| loan.status | 1 | interior | 0.0470 | 0.0113 | 0.24x | 0.132 |
| account.frequency | 1 | interior | 0.0216 | 0.0056 | 0.26x | 0.176 |
| client.year | 3 | interior | 0.0010 | 0.0005 | 0.50x | 0.161 |
| client.year | 25 | interior | 0.0115 | 0.0066 | 0.57x | 0.044 |
| client.year | 7 | interior | 0.0085 | 0.0049 | 0.58x | 0.386 |
| client.year | 19 | interior | 0.0102 | 0.0063 | 0.62x | 0.053 |
| client.year | 27 | interior | 0.0132 | 0.0084 | 0.64x | 0.047 |
| client.year | 70 | interior | 0.0062 | 0.0041 | 0.65x | 0.223 |
| client.year | 31 | interior | 0.0207 | 0.0137 | 0.66x | 0.062 |
| client.year | 75 | edge | 0.0002 | 0.0002 | 0.67x | 2.517 |
| client.year | 22 | interior | 0.0102 | 0.0068 | 0.67x | 0.045 |
| client.year | 18 | interior | 0.0082 | 0.0057 | 0.69x | 0.045 |

Retention is a ratio, so a few classes that *gained* mass drag the mean above 1 and hide the effect. Median retention by rarity, split by position:

| real share | interior (median retained) | edge (median retained) |
|---|---|---|
| < 0.5% | 1.67x (n=9) | 1.25x (n=2) |
| 0.5-2% | 1.00x (n=138) | 1.00x (n=5) |
| 2-10% | 0.99x (n=73) | 1.00x (n=10) |

- **No systematic effect.** Interior classes do not lose more mass than edge classes at matched rarity, and the width of a class's band in quantile-normal space is uncorrelated with how much of it survives (Spearman rho = -0.03, n=237). The very rarest classes tend to *gain* mass (median 1.67x), the opposite of absorption — rounding pulls neighbours in as often as it pushes them out.
- **But it bites hard on specific columns**, and unluckily on the ones that carry labels: `loan.status` code 1 (class B, the minority default outcome) retains 0.24x, `account.frequency` code 1 retains 0.26x, `trans.k_symbol` code 4 retains 0.14x. These are real and reproducible across all three seeds; they are simply not an instance of a general rule about interior codes.
- **And fix S1 shows it is not what breaks utility anyway.** Restoring every categorical marginal exactly — class B back to 24 rows, default rate 0.073 -> 0.113 — moved downstream AUROC by +0.0006 on card ownership and *down* on loan default. H2 is visible in the fidelity tables and irrelevant to the downstream result.

## A3 — The smallest tables get almost no gradient

Seed nodes are drawn uniformly over all non-dimension rows and the diffusion loss is
computed on seed nodes only, so per-step gradient signal is proportional to table size.

| table | rows | share of seed pool | seeds per 4096-row batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| trans | 792,600 | 97.846% | 4007.8 |
| order | 4,874 | 0.602% | 24.6 |
| client | 4,007 | 0.495% | 20.3 |
| disp | 4,007 | 0.495% | 20.3 |
| account | 3,375 | 0.417% | 17.1 |
| card | 672 | 0.083% | 3.4 |
| loan | 511 | 0.063% | 2.6 |

`trans` takes 4007.8 of every 4096 seeds; `loan` takes 2.6. Fix S3 re-weights this.


## A6 — Cardinality is the aggregate family the generator never learns

Generated by `downstream/diagnose_aggregates.py`. A2 asked *how far* the conditioning window
reaches; this asks *what kind* of neighbourhood information is missing inside it. The child-table
features `tasks.py` builds split into four families — counts (`*_n`), means and sums, spread and
extremes (std/min/max/last/span), and per-code share vectors — plus the row's own columns.

**Where the signal lives, on real data** (XGBoost, leakage-free split, 3 xgb seeds):

| feature set | cols | loan default | cols | card ownership |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| own row only | 24 | 0.6678 ± 0.0089 | 23 | 0.6756 ± 0.0027 |
| own + **count** | 26 | **0.8195 ± 0.0096** | 25 | 0.6878 ± 0.0067 |
| own + mean/sum | 31 | 0.8113 ± 0.0056 | 30 | **0.8641 ± 0.0027** |
| own + spread/extremes | 36 | 0.7914 ± 0.0057 | 35 | 0.8377 ± 0.0008 |
| own + code shares | 76 | 0.8800 ± 0.0108 | 69 | 0.7757 ± 0.0019 |
| everything | 97 | 0.8879 ± 0.0143 | 90 | 0.8736 ± 0.0011 |

**Two count columns carry two thirds of the loan task.** Adding `trans_n` and `order_n` to the
row's own 24 columns moves loan default from 0.668 to 0.820, out of a 0.888 ceiling. Card
ownership is the opposite: counts add 0.012, and its signal sits in the means and the spread of
the transaction rollup.

**How much of each family's label association survives generation** — mean of
`|corr_syn| / |corr_real|` over features whose real point-biserial correlation exceeds 0.02, seed
0. 1.00x is faithful; below is a collapsed association, above is an inflated one.

| variant | count | mean/sum | spread/extremes | code shares | own row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *loan default* | | | | | |
| baseline | **0.56x** | 0.89x | 1.50x | 0.88x | 0.90x |
| S2 · n_hops=2 | **0.63x** | 0.69x | 0.99x | 0.81x | 0.90x |
| S3 · balanced seeds | **0.34x** | 0.86x | 1.06x | 0.73x | 1.01x |
| S5 · both | 1.29x | 1.39x | 2.15x | 0.69x | 1.40x |
| *card ownership* | | | | | |
| baseline | **0.07x** | 0.07x | 0.17x | 0.29x | 0.34x |
| S2 · n_hops=2 | **0.07x** | 0.09x | 0.31x | 0.33x | 0.44x |
| S3 · balanced seeds | **0.07x** | 0.13x | 0.05x | 0.44x | 0.31x |
| S5 · both | **0.07x** | 0.36x | 0.40x | 0.50x | 0.45x |

**The count column does not move.** On card ownership it is 0.07x in every variant — baseline,
depth, balanced seeding, and both together. Every other family improves under S5 (mean/sum
0.07 → 0.36, spread 0.17 → 0.40, code shares 0.29 → 0.50, own row 0.34 → 0.45) and cardinality
alone stays exactly where it started. On loan default it is the worst-preserved family in three
of the four variants. Nothing tried so far touches it.

That is what `--num_neighbors` predicts. The GNN is handed a fixed number of children — 16 past
the first hop — so every aggregate it forms is a sample of constant size, and the true fan-out is
not merely noisy in its representation, it is *absent* from it. No amount of depth or gradient
rebalancing can recover a quantity the input never contained.

**This is what S10 supplies directly** (`--degree_features`): the exact per-relation child count,
attached to every node, at 1.11x the training step cost. It is the cheapest of the four
architecture changes and the one aimed at the only family that has resisted every previous fix.
S8 (multi-statistic aggregation) covers the second half of the picture — the means and spreads
that carry card ownership, still at 0.36x/0.40x after S5.

Caveat: a ratio above 1.0 is a distortion too, not a bonus. S5's loan row (1.29x count, 2.15x
spread) means those associations are *stronger* in the synthetic data than in the real database,
which is a different failure from a collapse and is not obviously benign — it is simply not the
failure that costs the most AUROC here.
